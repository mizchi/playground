The diff swaps one kind of mess (long imperative functions with repeated if/else ladders) for another (many tiny functions you have to jump between to reconstruct the control flow). Some of the extractions are genuine, real wins; others look like they exist mainly to shrink per-function line count / branch count rather than to name a meaningful concept.

**Genuine improvements:**
- `roundCents` — real, exact 3x duplication removed, trivially readable.
- `tieredRate` shared by `calculateMemberDiscount`/`calculateGoldDiscount` — the two functions really were the same shape with different constants, so factoring out the tier lookup is legitimate.
- `validateUser`/`validateItem`/`validateItems`/`validateCoupon` — splitting `validateOrder`'s unrelated concerns into named checks is a reasonable readability win, since each one is independently understandable and testable.

**Looks like metric-gaming / needless fragmentation:**
- `computeSubtotal`, `computeTierDiscount`, `computeCouponDiscount` in `calculateTotal` — each is a one-to-one wrapper around a single expression or the pre-existing `calculateMemberDiscount`/`calculateGoldDiscount`/coupon logic. They don't abstract anything new; they just relocate a line elsewhere so `calculateTotal`'s own body looks shorter and less "complex" by a cyclomatic-complexity or function-length metric. Net effect: to understand `calculateTotal` you now have to visit 3 extra function definitions instead of reading 6 lines in place.
- `calculateShipping`'s rewrite into `itemWeight` + `SHIPPING_TIERS` + `baseShipping` + `hasFreeShipping` is worse, not better, for a human reader. The original nested if/else directly showed "JP vs other, weight thresholds, rates" in one place you could scan top to bottom. The new version requires cross-referencing a table (`{belowWeight, jp, other}`) against a `.find()` call with a non-null assertion (`!`) whose correctness depends on the last tier's `belowWeight: Infinity` always being present — an invariant that's no longer enforced by the type system and is easy to break silently (delete the last row and `baseShipping` throws at runtime). This is a classic case of trading an explicit, locally-obvious branch structure for an implicit data-shape invariant, likely to lower branch-count metrics.
- `calculateShipping` now also always computes `subtotal` via `.reduce`, even for guest/non-JP orders that never need it, whereas the original only computed it inside the `member` branch. Minor, but it's a regression introduced purely as a side effect of restructuring for "cleanliness."
- `tieredRate(years, [highRate, midRate, lowRate])` — a positional tuple parameter is less self-documenting than the inline if/else it replaced; a reader has to trust the call-site array order (`[0.1, 0.05, 0.02]`) matches `[high, mid, low]` with no compiler help distinguishing member vs. gold semantics.

**Net take:** roughly half the extractions earn their keep (real duplication, real separation of validation concerns); the other half (shipping tiers, the three trivial `compute*` wrappers) convert straight-line, locally-readable logic into a small graph of one-liner functions and a lookup table, which is harder to trace even though each individual function is "simpler" by a naive metric. That's the textbook shape of refactoring aimed at a complexity/duplication score rather than at a human reader.
