**VERDICT: better** — the refactor removes real duplication (rounding, the JP/other shipping ladder, the two near-identical discount-tier chains) and turns validation into a clean composition (`validateOrder` is now a one-line summary of its sub-checks). Net effect is a smaller surface to scan and less risk of the two discount functions drifting out of sync, which they were clearly prone to before.

Caveats / things that look like they exist mainly to shrink a complexity number rather than to help a reader:

- `calculateDiscount` and `calculateCouponDiscount` are each called exactly once, from `calculateTotal`, with no reuse anywhere else. Splitting them out doesn't reduce total complexity, it just relocates it and forces the reader to jump between four small functions to reconstruct what used to be one linear ~15-line function. This reads like extraction to get `calculateTotal`'s own cyclomatic/line count under a threshold, not a genuine abstraction.
- `validateItem` vs `validateItems` vs `validateUser` vs `validateCoupon`: defensible as "one function per field," but note none of them is reused either — it's the same single-caller-extraction pattern, just more palatable because the composition (`[...validateUser(...), ...validateItems(...), ...validateCoupon(...)]`) reads well. Borderline, but I'd keep it since the result is genuinely more scannable.
- `discountRate(years, tiers)` / `SHIPPING_RATES.find(r => weight < r.limit)`: legitimate dedup (two real call sites), but it trades explicit, obviously-correct if/else chains for tables whose correctness depends on an *unenforced ordering invariant* (tiers must be sorted descending by `min`; shipping rows must be ascending by `limit` for `find` to pick the right one). That's a real maintainability regression hiding under a "more generic" refactor — a future contributor adding a tier in the wrong position gets silently wrong behavior with no type or runtime check to catch it. Original code couldn't have this bug class.

Bottom line: worth keeping, but I'd inline `calculateDiscount`/`calculateCouponDiscount` back into `calculateTotal` (no real justification for the split) and add a comment or assertion documenting the sort-order requirement on the two tier tables.
