VERDICT: better

**report.ts** is a clean, genuine dedup: three functions with byte-identical accumulation loops (differing only in the key expression) collapse into one loop plus three one-line strategy callbacks. This is a real win — a future change to the summing logic (e.g. switching to `Map`, handling negative quantities, adding a total row) now happens in one place instead of three. The `(_order, item) => item.category` signature is a minor wart (unused param) but a normal and acceptable cost of a shared callback shape.

**order.ts** is more marginal. The two original functions were each two lines, differing only in the tier literal and the tiers table. Extracting `calculateTierDiscount(user, subtotal, tier, tiers)` is correct and not unreasonable, but it's dedup-for-dedup's-sake on code that was already trivially readable — you've traded "two obvious 2-line functions" for "one helper + two call sites," which is a wash for readability and arguably adds a layer of indirection you now have to jump through to see what `calculateMemberDiscount` does. If a future tier needed different rounding or an extra eligibility check, this abstraction would have to be unwound anyway.

Neither hunk shows signs of metric-gaming (no artificial splitting into meaningless micro-functions, no fake parameterization that doesn't actually share logic, no renaming/reformatting just to dodge a duplication scanner). The report.ts change is a solid maintainability improvement; the order.ts change is defensible but low-value — net effect across the diff is a modest improvement.
