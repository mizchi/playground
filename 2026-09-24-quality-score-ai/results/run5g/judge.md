**VERDICT: better**

The refactor is mostly legitimate: `validateOrder` is genuinely clearer split into `validateUser`/`validateItems`/`validateItem`/`validateCoupon` (removes nesting, no behavior change), and `report.ts`'s `summarizeBy` collapses three copy-pasted loops into one real abstraction with an obvious `keyFor` callback — that's textbook DRY, not metric gaming. Replacing the shipping weight/fee if-chains with `CATEGORY_WEIGHT_FACTORS` / `SHIPPING_RATES` tables is also a fair trade: adding a country or category is now a data-table edit instead of another branch, and I traced the bracket logic against the original and it's behavior-preserving.

One spot leans toward gaming a duplication/complexity checker rather than serving readers: `rateForYears(years, tiers)` used by both `calculateMemberDiscount` and `calculateGoldDiscount`. The two original functions were nearly identical in *shape* but encode distinct, self-explanatory business rules (member vs. gold loyalty tiers). Collapsing them into a generic loop over anonymous tuples like `[[5, 0.1], [2, 0.05], [0, 0.02]]` removes the "if years >= 5 → 0.1" phrasing that made the rule legible at the call site, and forces the reader to mentally re-sort/interpret bare number pairs (and trust that the list is ordered descending and terminated with a `0` catch-all — nothing enforces that). This reads like it exists to make two similar-but-not-truly-duplicate blocks stop tripping a duplication detector, at a small but real cost to at-a-glance clarity. It's minor and still net-positive since the dedup is small, but it's the one place I'd push back on in review and suggest keeping the explicit if/else (or at least naming the tuple fields via a small interface) instead.

Everything else — `roundMoney`, `orderSubtotal`, `tierDiscount`, `couponDiscountFor`, `hasFreeShipping` — are honest, well-named extractions that reduce nesting and duplication without obscuring intent.
