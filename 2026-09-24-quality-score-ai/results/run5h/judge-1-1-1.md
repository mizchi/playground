**Genuine improvements:** `round2`, `itemsSubtotal`, and the `validateUser/validateItem/validateItems/validateCoupon` split are honest wins — pure functions, no shared state, names match intent, and validation composes cleanly. `discountForUser`/`couponDiscountFor` are reasonable extractions too.

**Regressions that look like metric-gaming, not design:**

1. **`tieredRate` and `tieredFee` are the same algorithm ("find the value for the first matching threshold") implemented twice with gratuitously different conventions** — one checks `years >= min` on a descending list with the fallback baked in as `[0, rate]`; the other checks `weight < max` on an ascending list with the fallback passed as a separate argument. There's no functional reason for the divergence — it reads like two copies were kept just different enough to dodge a duplicate-code detector, rather than being unified into one `firstMatch(value, tiers, fallback)` helper. This is worse than the original, which was at least honest about being two parallel if/else chains.

2. **Implicit ordering contracts with no enforcement.** `tieredRate([[5, 0.1], [2, 0.05], [0, 0.02]])` only works because the list is sorted descending and checked with `>=`; `tieredFee` only works sorted ascending and checked with `<`. Nothing in the type (`[number, number][]`) or a comment says so. A future edit that appends a tier out of order, or copies one convention into the other function's call site, silently produces wrong discounts/shipping fees with no compiler or runtime signal. The original nested if/else was more verbose but impossible to get subtly wrong this way.

3. **Over-fragmentation of `calculateShipping`.** It's now spread across `itemsSubtotal`, `isFreeShipping`, `totalWeight`, `itemWeight`, `CATEGORY_WEIGHT_PER_UNIT`, `baseShippingFee`, `JP_SHIPPING_TIERS`/`DEFAULT_SHIPPING_TIERS`, and `tieredFee` — seven indirections to trace one business rule. Each function is individually trivial (low per-function cyclomatic complexity, short line count), but understanding "how is shipping computed" now requires jumping through the whole chain instead of reading one linear block. That's the classic signature of slicing code to please a per-function complexity/LOC metric rather than to help a reader.

Net effect: less line-level duplication, but the two "generic" tier-lookup functions substitute a readability problem (verbose repetition) for a correctness-risk problem (fragile, undocumented ordering assumptions) — and the shipping logic is harder to hold in your head despite being "simpler" by any static metric. I'd push back on this PR and ask for one shared tiered-lookup helper with an explicit, type-enforced tier direction, and for the shipping helpers to be collapsed back toward 2-3 functions.
