**VERDICT: worse**

The validation split (`validateUser`/`validateItem`/`validateItems`/`validateCoupon`) and the `itemsSubtotal`/`round2` extraction are genuine, welcome deduplication — real repeated logic, extracted cleanly with no behavior change.

But `tieredRate`/`tieredFee` are the tell. The original discount/shipping tiers were explicit `if/else if` chains where the comparison and the threshold sat right next to each other and were trivially readable. They're now collapsed into generic loops over `[number, number][]` tuples like `[[5, 0.1], [2, 0.05], [0, 0.02]]` or `[[1, 300], [5, 600], [20, 1200]]` — you can no longer tell from the call site what the two numbers in each pair mean, whether the comparison is `>=` or `<`, or that correctness depends on the tiers being pre-sorted (first match wins, not best match). That's an implicit invariant with no enforcement or comment. Worse, the two helpers encode the "no match" case inconsistently — `tieredRate` folds it into a `0`-threshold tuple in the list, while `tieredFee` uses a separate `fallback` parameter — so a maintainer has to hold two different mental models for what's conceptually the same pattern.

This reads like collapsing nested if/else branches specifically to shrink a cyclomatic-complexity or duplication score, not because the resulting code communicates intent better. The line count dropped and the "duplicate if/else shape" disappeared, but a future engineer editing a discount tier now has to reverse-engineer tuple semantics and ordering rules instead of just reading a threshold chain — a net loss for readability and a new source of subtle bugs.
