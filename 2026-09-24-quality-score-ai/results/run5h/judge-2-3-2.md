**VERDICT: better** — but with one flag worth fixing.

Most of the extraction is genuine, not cosmetic: `roundCents` removes three copies of the same rounding expression, `subtotalOf` removes a duplicated reduce that's now shared between `calculateTotal` and `hasFreeShipping`, and splitting `validateOrder` into `validateUser`/`validateItem`/`validateItems`/`validateCoupon` turns a deeply nested pyramid into named, independently-readable rules — that's a real maintainability win (e.g. "furniture qty > 10" is now locatable by name instead of by scanning indentation levels). `discountFor`/`couponDiscountFor`/`hasFreeShipping` also give names to branches that previously had none, which helps `calculateTotal` read as a straight-line story of the pricing pipeline.

**The one thing that smells like metric-gaming: `tieredRate`.** It merges two *unrelated* domain concepts — "discount rate by loyalty years" and "shipping price by parcel weight" — into a single generic helper purely because both were "find the first threshold that value exceeds, descending" loops. That's exactly the kind of structural-shape-only DRYing a duplication checker rewards but a reader doesn't benefit from:

- The parameter names are literally `years: number, tiers: Array<[minYears: number, rate: number]>`, yet it's called from `baseShippingRate(weight, ...)` with weight, not years. Anyone reading `tieredRate(weight, tiers)` has to go check the implementation to realize "years" is a leftover misnomer — a real readability regression, not just cosmetic.
- The original shipping logic used exclusive upper bounds (`weight < 5 → 600`) while discount logic used inclusive lower bounds (`years >= 5 → 0.1`). Forcing shipping into the same `>=`, descending-tier shape required re-deriving equivalent boundaries (`[5, 1200], [1, 600], [0, 300]`) — behavior-preserving here, but it's exactly the kind of transformation that's easy to get subtly wrong under refactor pressure, for zero conceptual benefit (shipping cost and loyalty discount aren't the same thing just because both happen to be step functions).

If you keep `tieredRate`, rename it to something value-agnostic (`tieredLookup(value, tiers)`) and drop the `years`/`minYears` naming — or just don't share it across the two domains at all and accept the small duplication; it's cheaper to read than the indirection.
