**VERDICT: better**

The refactor is a net improvement: `roundMoney` and `subtotalOf` eliminate real duplicated logic (subtotal was being manually re-summed in the shipping calc); the shipping/weight tables replace parallel if/else chains with data lookups that are obviously correct at a glance; and the validation functions (`validateUser`/`validateItem`/`validateItems`/`validateCoupon`) turn a deeply nested, stateful `errors.push` blob into small pure functions that compose via `flatMap`/spread — each one is independently readable and testable. `tierDiscount` and `couponDiscountAmount` similarly make `calculateTotal` read as a clean pipeline instead of a wall of branching.

One spot smells like a metric-driven extraction rather than a genuine readability win: **`tieredRate(years, atFive, atTwo, base)`**. The two callers now read as `tieredRate(user.years, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02)` and `tieredRate(user.years, 0.2, 0.15, 0.1)` — four positional numeric args with no argument labels at the call site. In the original code, `rate = 0.1` for `years >= 5` was self-evident inline; now the reader has to jump to the helper and map "atFive/atTwo/base" back onto the call-site's third positional float to know which number means what. This looks like collapsing two structurally-similar (but semantically distinct) blocks purely to shrink a duplication-detector's count, at a small cost to clarity — a named-parameter object or just leaving the two if/else chains inline would have been safer. It's minor and doesn't flip the overall verdict, but it's the one piece I'd push back on in review.

Everything else — constant lookup tables, `roundMoney`, the validation split, `hasFreeShipping` — reflects genuine duplication removal and clearer intent, not metric gaming.
