VERDICT: worse

The extracted `weightBasedRate` helper takes four positional numeric parameters (`under1, under5, under20, over20`) with no way to tell at a call site which number means what — `weightBasedRate(weight, 300, 600, 1200, 3000)` is strictly less readable than the original inline tiered `if` chain, where the rate for each threshold was visible right next to the threshold. This is a classic case of gaming a duplication/complexity metric: the two branches (JP vs. default) genuinely were near-duplicates, but collapsing them into a function with four opaque positional args trades a small, easy-to-read duplication for a harder-to-read indirection. A reader now has to jump to the helper definition and count parameter positions to know that the third argument is the "under 20" rate. It would be better either to leave the duplication (it's only 4 lines and self-documenting), or to use a small lookup table / object literal keyed by country (e.g., `{JP: [300,600,1200,3000], default: [...]}`) with named tiers, which would reduce duplication without sacrificing clarity.
